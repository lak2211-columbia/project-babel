The Commission internal use
of Artificial Intelligence
October 2025
Leading by example: The European Commission is striving to become a modern, responsible and adaptive
institution by implementing robust AI initiatives under the AI@EC Communication. These initiatives ensure
that AI development and use within the Commission are lawful, safe, and trustworthy.
Driving responsible AI uptake
• Clear Guidelines: The Commission has developed comprehensive guidelines for AI use, development,
procurement, and cybersecurity for its Staff. This includes resources on generative AI, data management,
supercomputing, sustainability, and open-source development, alongside practical tools like FAQs on
intellectual property and an AI guide for managers.
• Risk-Based AI Register: AI systems in development or use are classified based on a risk-driven
methodology aligned with the AI Act, tracked in an internal register to ensure compliance and facilitate
reporting.
• Ongoing Oversight: The Interservice Steering Group on AI regularly monitors internal AI initiatives, such
as the development of new tools, training, corporate communication and guidelines, ensuring they align
with the Commission’s goal of fostering the use of safe and trustworthy AI.
Empowering an AI-Skilled Workforce:
• Training and Community: The Commission fosters AI literacy through training programmes, the AI@
EC portal for knowledge sharing, the AI@EC Network for community practice, and peer learning via the AI
Champions network. An internal survey helps identify gaps in AI tool adoption.
Some examples of how AI helps Commission Staff:
• Support with repetitive or administrative tasks.
• Support evidence-based policy making by helping staff navigate vast amounts of information.
• Support multilingualism.
GPT@EC: An in-house generative AI tool
available to all staff, with API access to enhance
other systems. Its code is expected for a first
open-source release in 2026.
Language tools such as e-Translation and others,
provide new capabilities thanks to new generative AI
features.
AI4DRPM (AI for Digital-Ready Policy-Making):
Digital statements are now attached to every
legislative proposal. AI4DRPM is a system analysing
draft legislation, detecting and classifying digital
relevance and producing a draft digital statement
expected for every legislative proposal. Policy officers
remain in full control, finalising the output.
