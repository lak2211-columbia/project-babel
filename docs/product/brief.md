# Product Brief

## Core thesis

AI governance divergence matters along two dimensions:

1. What governments require in practice
2. Why governments believe those requirements are necessary

Countries that align on regulatory mechanics but diverge on risk framing may appear coordinated while actually sitting on unstable ground. Countries that align on risk framing but diverge on implementation may be better candidates for future convergence.

## Core users

- Policy researchers
- AI governance analysts
- Standards and safety teams
- Journalists and think tank researchers

## Primary research questions

- Which jurisdictions are converging or fragmenting on concrete AI governance requirements?
- Which jurisdictions are talking about AI risk in compatible or incompatible ways?
- Does similarity in framing predict future policy convergence better than similarity in current rules?

## MVP

The first usable release should support:

- A source registry for official government AI governance documents
- Structured extraction for document type, binding status, covered applications, requirements, enforcement, and implementation dates
- Risk framing classification across a fixed codebook
- Basic comparative views by jurisdiction and year
- A first divergence index built from both policy structure and framing distribution

## Non-goals for v1

- Broad media coverage ingestion
- Predictive forecasting beyond the divergence index
- Fully automated public release without quality checks
